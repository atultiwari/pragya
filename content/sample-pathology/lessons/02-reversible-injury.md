## Cellular swelling

The earliest change in most forms of injury is **cellular swelling** (hydropic change). When ATP falls, the Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase in the plasma membrane slows. Sodium and water then enter the cell faster than they are pumped out.

Under the light microscope, swollen cells look pale, and small clear vacuoles may appear in the cytoplasm.

## Fatty change

Fatty change is the accumulation of lipid vacuoles in the cytoplasm. It is seen mostly in cells that handle fat, above all **hepatocytes**, and occurs with toxins, alcohol and hypoxia.

## Ultrastructural changes

1. Blebbing and blunting of microvilli at the plasma membrane
2. Swelling of mitochondria and of the endoplasmic reticulum
3. Detachment of ribosomes from the endoplasmic reticulum
4. Myelin figures: whorls of phospholipid from damaged membranes
5. Clumping of nuclear chromatin

> [!MEMORY] Remember
> Reversible injury = swelling + fatty change, with the membrane and nucleus still intact.
