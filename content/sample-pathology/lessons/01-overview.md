## From stress to injury

Cells live in a narrow range of conditions. When a stress is mild or slow, they **adapt** by changing size, number or type. When the stress outruns their ability to adapt, they are **injured**. Up to a point, injury is reversible; beyond it, the cell dies.

![Normal, adapted, reversibly injured and dead cell](figures/injury-spectrum.svg "The spectrum from adaptation to cell death")

## Common causes

- **Oxygen deprivation**: hypoxia, and ischaemia (loss of blood supply), the most frequent cause in practice
- **Physical agents**: trauma, extremes of temperature, radiation
- **Chemicals and drugs**, including therapeutic drugs at toxic doses
- **Infectious agents**: viruses, bacteria, fungi, parasites
- **Immunological reactions**: autoimmunity and hypersensitivity
- **Genetic abnormalities** and **nutritional imbalances**

## Reversible or irreversible?

| Feature         | Reversible injury                    | Irreversible injury                |
| --------------- | ------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------- |
| Cell volume     | Swollen                              | Swollen, then fragmented           |
| Plasma membrane | Blebs, but intact                    | Breached                           |
| Mitochondria    | Swollen                              | Swollen with amorphous densities   |
| Nucleus         | Normal or slightly clumped chromatin | Pyknosis, karyorrhexis, karyolysis |

> [!CLINICAL] Why this matters at the bedside
> Enzymes released through a breached membrane are clinically measurable. Cardiac troponins in blood are evidence that myocardial cells have died.
