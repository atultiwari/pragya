## Nuclear signs of cell death

- **Pyknosis**: the nucleus shrinks and stains darkly
- **Karyorrhexis**: the pyknotic nucleus breaks into fragments
- **Karyolysis**: the chromatin fades as DNA is digested

## Patterns of necrosis

| Pattern      | Typical setting               | Key feature                                  |
| ------------ | ----------------------------- | -------------------------------------------- |
| Coagulative  | Infarcts of heart and kidney  | Tissue outline preserved for days            |
| Liquefactive | Brain infarcts, abscesses     | Tissue digested to a liquid mass             |
| Caseous      | Tuberculosis                  | Cheese-like, amorphous centre of a granuloma |
| Fat          | Acute pancreatitis            | Chalky deposits from fat saponification      |
| Fibrinoid    | Immune damage to vessel walls | Bright pink, fibrin-like deposits            |

> [!EXAM] Common viva question
> Why does a brain infarct liquefy instead of coagulating? Hydrolytic enzymes released by dying cells and by inflammatory cells digest tissue that has little connective-tissue stroma to hold its shape. The high lipid content of brain tissue is often cited as well.

## Apoptosis

Apoptosis is a regulated, energy-dependent programme of cell death. The cell shrinks, the chromatin condenses, and the cell breaks into **apoptotic bodies** that phagocytes clear without inflammation.

| Feature            | Necrosis            | Apoptosis                    |
| ------------------ | ------------------- | ---------------------------- |
| Cell size          | Enlarged (swelling) | Reduced (shrinkage)          |
| Plasma membrane    | Disrupted           | Intact, with altered surface |
| Inflammation       | Frequent            | Absent                       |
| Physiological role | Always pathological | Often physiological          |
